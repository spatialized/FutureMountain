## Level 3

### Opening
Looking at climate can only do so much to explain ecosystem processes and wildfire risk. Another important factor to consider is vegetation, and how that couples with climate in different locations to create differences in vegetation cover, vegetation behavior, and vegetation response to fire.

Within FM there are 5 patches: high elevation, low elevation, north-facing patch, south-facing patch, and a riparian patch. Tap on each to explore some of the following characteristics while keeping note of:

Vegetation height 
Productivity (Net Primary Productivity) 
Plant Respiration 
Plant Transpiration 
Evapotranspiration
Vegetation Water Access 
Rain accumulation 
Types of vegetation and structures 

Click on the "ready" when ready to answer some questions in order to proceed


### Zone Identification

**Select the title of the zone that each description is referring to.**

#### 3.1 North

**Description:**

This zone has the largest year-to-year variation in plants' primary productivity (or energy generation), above-ground biomass, respiration, and transpiration, characterized by an average elevation within the watershed.

**Answer:** North

---

#### 3.2 South

**Description:**

This zone has the lowest amount of above-ground biomass and is considered the least productive in terms of net primary productivity because this zone is mostly composed of grass.

This zone receives more solar radiation and has the most direct sunlight of all of the zones. More sunlight and radiation can lead to increased temperatures, increased evaporation, and less water available in the soil for plants.

**Answer:** South

---

#### 3.3 Riparian

**Description:**

This zone is one where the plants have access to water from a stream.

This zone is the only zone with coast live oaks. Coast live oaks average 10–15 meters in height, compared to chaparral, where mature shrubs average 2–4 meters.

Water is not a limiting factor in this zone, leading to increased biomass and potentially taller plants.

**Answer:** Riparian

---

#### 3.4 Low

**Description:**

This zone has, on average, a lower above-ground biomass for the chaparral shrubs.

In this zone, rainfall does not determine how much access the vegetation has to water, as water naturally travels "downslope" to lower-elevation patches like this one.

It can also be categorized as the least "thirsty" for its low transpiration levels.

**Answer:** Low

---

#### 3.5 High

**Description:**

This zone has the most above-ground biomass in comparison to the other zones and consistent vegetation height, as no fire or other disturbances occur at this elevation level in any of our simulations.

The amount of access the vegetation has to water is very dependent on the amount of rainfall this zone accumulates; however, it does accumulate the most rainfall out of all the zones.

It also has the highest evapotranspiration and potentially the most ecosystem functionality.

**Answer:** High
